# Renter-Friendly Security Systems 2026: No-Drill, No-Contract Picks

A security system is the one smart home purchase where renters get told "no" the most. Every traditional system wants a three-year contract, a professional install, and holes in your door frames.

That advice is out of date. Modern DIY systems mount with adhesive strips, run on battery, and cost nothing per month if you skip professional monitoring. We installed three of them in a rental apartment over six weeks and then removed them to check the walls.

## What Makes a Security System Renter-Safe

Four things, in order of how badly they can cost you:

1. **No contract.** A multi-year monitoring contract survives your lease. If you move somewhere the system does not cover, you still pay.
2. **No drilling.** Door and window sensors on adhesive strips come off clean. Sensors screwed into frames do not.
3. **Battery or plug-in, not wired.** You cannot hire an electrician in a rental, and you cannot rewire a panel.
4. **Portable.** When you move, the whole system should fit in one box and work at the next place.

Everything below meets all four.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will my landlord know I installed a security system?**
The sensors are small and stick to door frames. There are no holes and no wiring. In most leases this is not an "alteration" — but read your lease, and see our [guide to talking to your landlord](/articles/ask-landlord-about-smart-home.html).

**Do I need the monitoring plan?**
No. All four recommended systems work as self-monitored alarms with free app alerts. Monitoring is what calls the police for you when you are not home.

**What happens when I move?**
You unplug the base station, peel off the sensors, and reinstall at the new place in about 20 minutes. Adhesive strips are reusable if you buy fresh 3M command strips.

**Do these work without internet?**
The alarm sounds locally and the sensors still trigger. App alerts need internet, and cellular backup (SimpliSafe) covers that gap.

**Can I use a security system in an apartment with a shared entry?**
Yes. Put a contact sensor on your own door and a motion sensor in your hallway. Skip anything on the building's front door — that is the landlord's.
