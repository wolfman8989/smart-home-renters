## Camera Placement Guide for Renters

### Indoor Cameras
- **Living room:** Corner, high angle, covering entry points
- **Bedroom:** Nightstand or shelf, facing door
- **Kitchen:** Counter or shelf, covering entry
- **Pet area:** Low angle, covering pet bed/food area

### Outdoor Cameras
- **Front door:** Above door, pointing outward
- **Balcony:** Mounted on railing or wall (adhesive)
- **Window:** Suction cup mount on glass
- **Parking:** Window or balcony, pointing at vehicle

### Doorbell Cameras
- **Peephole:** Replace existing peephole (no tools)
- **Adhesive mount:** Stick to door or doorframe
- **No-drill mount:** Ring's no-drill mount for battery doorbells

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can renters install security cameras without landlord permission?**
Yes, for most battery-powered and plug-in cameras. No-drill mounts and adhesive installations don't require landlord permission. Always check your lease for specific restrictions.

**Do security cameras work in apartments with poor Wi-Fi?**
Wi-Fi cameras need solid 2.4GHz signal. For thick walls, consider a Wi-Fi extender or cameras with local storage (Eufy) that don't depend on cloud.

**Can I take security cameras when I move?**
Yes. Everything in this guide is portable. Unplug, unstick, pack, and reinstall at your new place.

**Do I need a subscription for security cameras?**
Not always. Eufy and Wyze offer local storage or free cloud storage. Ring and Blink require subscriptions for video history.

**Are security cameras legal in apartments?**
Yes, for your own unit. You cannot point cameras at common areas, neighbors' units, or private spaces. Always check local laws and your lease.
