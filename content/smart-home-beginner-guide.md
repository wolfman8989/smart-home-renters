# Smart Home for Apartments: Complete Beginner's Guide (2026)

You're renting. You can't drill holes, run wires, or modify the electrical. But you still want voice control, automated lights, and a home that feels smart. Good news: almost everything in a modern smart home is renter-friendly.

This guide walks you through building a smart apartment from zero to "wow, this is actually useful" in four weekends, spending $50-200 total.

## What You Need Before You Start

Before buying anything, check three things:

**1. Your Wi-Fi.** Most smart devices need 2.4GHz Wi-Fi. If your router is dual-band (most are), you're fine. If you're on a shared building network (common in student housing), some devices won't connect. Ask your landlord or check your router settings.

**2. Your lease.** Read it. Look for clauses about "modifications," "alterations," or "fixtures." Most leases prohibit drilling and permanent changes. Everything in this guide is adhesive-mounted, plug-in, or screw-in (bulbs) — all removable. But if your lease says "no modifications of any kind," even adhesive might be a gray area. When in doubt, ask.

**3. Your outlets.** Count them. Smart plugs need outlets. If your apartment has 3 outlets per room, you can add 3 smart plugs. If you have 1 outlet behind a couch, you'll need a power strip first.

## The Four-Weekend Plan

### Weekend 1: Lighting ($20-40)

Start with lights. They're the most visible, most satisfying, and cheapest smart home upgrade.

**Buy:** TP-Link Kasa KL125 smart bulbs (2-pack, $20) or Govee LED strip ($15).

**Install:** Screw in the bulbs or stick the LED strip. Download the app. Connect to Wi-Fi. Done in 10 minutes.

**What you get:** Voice control ("Alexa, turn off the lights"), color scenes, scheduled on/off, and the feeling that your apartment just got 10x better.

**Pro tip:** Put smart bulbs in lamps, not ceiling fixtures. Lamps are portable, ceiling fixtures require switches. If you put a smart bulb in a ceiling fixture controlled by a wall switch, someone will turn off the switch and the bulb goes dumb.

### Weekend 2: Plugs ($12-35)

Smart plugs are the second-best investment. They turn any dumb device into a smart one.

**Buy:** Wyze Plug 2-pack ($12) for budget, or TP-Link Kasa EP25 4-pack ($35) for energy monitoring.

**Install:** Plug in, connect to app, done. 2 minutes per plug.

**What to plug in:**
- Lamp (if you didn't do smart bulbs)
- Fan (schedule it to turn on before you get home)
- Coffee maker (schedule it to brew at 7am)
- Space heater (schedule it, but never leave unattended)
- TV/entertainment center (cut phantom power draw at night)

**Pro tip:** Don't put smart plugs on things you use frequently by hand. If you have to walk to the plug to turn on a lamp, the smart plug is pointless. Put smart plugs on things you want to automate (fans, heaters, coffee makers) or things you want to voice-control from bed (lamps).

### Weekend 3: Voice Control ($0-50)

If you don't have a smart speaker yet, get one. Voice control is what makes smart home feel like magic.

**Buy:** Amazon Echo Dot (5th Gen, $50) or Echo Dot with Clock ($60). If you're on a budget, the previous-gen Echo Dot goes on sale for $25-35 regularly.

**Setup:** Plug in, download Alexa app, connect to Wi-Fi. Then discover your smart devices (Alexa finds them automatically if they're on the same network).

**What you get:** "Alexa, turn off the bedroom light." "Alexa, start the coffee." "Alexa, good night" (turns off all lights, locks the door if you have a smart lock, sets the thermostat).

**Pro tip:** Put the Echo Dot in the room you spend the most time in. If that's the bedroom, put it there. Voice control from bed is the single best smart home experience.

### Weekend 4: One "Wow" Device ($30-100)

By now you have lights, plugs, and voice control. Weekend 4 is about adding one device that makes you say "wow."

- **Smart lock ($89-129):** Never carry keys again. Auto-unlock when you arrive. Temporary codes for guests. See our [no-drill smart lock guide](/articles/no-drill-smart-locks).
- **Video doorbell ($60-180):** See who's at the door from your phone. No wiring needed with battery models. See our [no-drill doorbell guide](/articles/no-drill-doorbell).
- **AC controller ($49-120):** Turn your dumb window AC into a smart AC. Schedule, geofence, voice control. See our [smart thermostat guide](/articles/smart-thermostats-renters).
- **Security camera ($25-60):** Indoor camera for peace of mind. Plug in, point, done. See our [renter security camera guide](/articles/renter-security-cameras).

Pick one. Don't buy all four. Master one device before adding another.

## The Renter's Rules

Follow these and you'll never have a problem with your landlord:

**1. Nothing permanent.** No drilling, no wiring, no screwing into walls. Adhesive, plug-in, and screw-in (bulbs) only. If it leaves a hole or a wire, don't do it.

**2. Everything comes with you.** If you can't pack it in a box and take it to your next apartment, don't buy it. Smart switches, hardwired thermostats, and built-in speakers are for homeowners.

**3. Check your lease.** Read it. Look for "modifications," "alterations," "fixtures," and "landlord consent." If you're unsure about a device, ask your landlord in writing. Most landlords don't care about smart bulbs and plugs. Some care about smart locks.

**4. Keep the original.** If you replace a dumb bulb with a smart one, keep the original bulb. When you move out, put the original back. Same for any device you temporarily install.

**5. Test removal before committing.** Before mounting anything with adhesive, test on a small area. Command strips are great but can pull paint if you yank them. Always warm adhesive with a hair dryer before removing.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

**Buying too much at once.** You don't need 15 devices on day one. Start with 2-3, learn how they work, then expand. Most people who buy a 10-device starter kit end up using 3 of them.

**Ignoring 2.4GHz.** Most smart devices only work on 2.4GHz Wi-Fi. If your router broadcasts a single combined network (2.4 + 5GHz on the same SSID), some devices fail to connect. Check your router settings and create a separate 2.4GHz network if needed.

**Choosing the wrong ecosystem.** Alexa, Google Home, and Apple HomeKit don't always play nice together. Pick one and stick with it. If you have an iPhone, consider HomeKit. If you have an Echo, go Alexa. If you have a Google Nest speaker, go Google Home.

**Forgetting about the app.** Every smart device has an app. That's 5-10 apps on your phone. It's annoying but manageable. Use a smart home hub app (Alexa, Google Home, or Apple Home) to control everything from one place.

**Not checking compatibility.** Smart thermostats need a C-wire. Smart switches need a neutral wire. Smart blinds need specific rod types. Check compatibility before buying, not after.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start a smart home in a rental?**
$12 for a Wyze Plug 2-pack. That's it. You can start with one smart plug and expand from there. Most people spend $50-150 in their first month and stop there.

**Do I need to tell my landlord?**
For plug-in devices (plugs, bulbs, speakers, cameras), no. For adhesive-mounted devices (smart locks, doorbells, blinds), it depends on your lease. When in doubt, ask. Most landlords don't care about devices that don't leave marks.

**What if I move?**
Everything in this guide is portable. Unplug, unscrew (bulbs), peel (adhesive), and pack. Reinstall at your new place in an afternoon. The only thing you lose is the time it takes to set up again.

**Which ecosystem should I choose?**
Alexa if you have an Echo. Google Home if you have a Nest speaker. Apple HomeKit if you're all-Apple. Don't mix ecosystems — pick one and stick with it.

**Are smart home devices secure?**
Reputable brands (TP-Link, Wyze, Amazon, Google, Apple, SwitchBot) use encryption and regular security updates. Avoid no-name brands from random sellers. Keep firmware updated. Use a strong Wi-Fi password.

**Can smart home devices work without internet?**
Some can. TP-Link Kasa plugs and bulbs work on your local network without internet (schedules still run). Voice control requires internet. Automations (routines) require internet. If your internet goes down, devices still work manually (flip the switch, press the button).
