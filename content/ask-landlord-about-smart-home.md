# How to Ask Your Landlord About Smart Home Devices (With Exact Wording)

Most renters either never ask (and worry) or ask badly (and get a no they did not need). This guide fixes both.

The core insight: **you almost never need to ask about the devices in this site.** A smart plug, a battery camera on a shelf, and a clip-on curtain robot are not alterations. You only need a conversation when you are changing something the landlord owns.

## First: Read Your Lease for These Four Phrases

Search your lease for these. They are the only clauses that matter.

| Phrase | What it usually means | Risk level |
|---|---|---|
| "No alterations without written consent" | Holes, fixtures, paint | Low for clip-on devices |
| "Tenant shall not change locks" | The deadbolt is theirs | Medium — inside-half swaps are usually fine |
| "No fixtures attached to walls" | Shelves, mounts, brackets | Low if you use adhesive |
| "Landlord may enter to inspect" | They can look | Low — be tidy and honest |

If your lease has all four, you can still install plugs, shelf cameras, clip-on locks, LED strips, and an IR blaster for the AC. Those are not alterations by any reasonable reading.

## The Three Tiers of Device

### Tier 1 — No permission needed

Nothing is attached to the property, nothing is drilled, nothing is replaced.

- Smart plugs
- Smart bulbs
- Battery cameras on a shelf
- Clip-on curtain robots
- IR blaster / hub on a shelf
- Adhesive LED strips
- Adhesive contact sensors on a door frame
- Portable heaters, fans, anything you own

You can buy these today. Do not ask. Asking creates an opportunity for a no.

### Tier 2 — Tell them, do not ask

You are touching something the landlord owns, but reversibly and without damage.

- Replacing the **inside half** of a deadbolt (Wyze Lock, SwitchBot Lock, August)
- A no-drill doorbell bracket on your own door
- Adhesive-mounted cameras on the outside of your own door

Send a short message, keep a copy. You are notifying, not requesting. Wording below.

### Tier 3 — Ask in writing and get a yes

You are modifying a fixture or the building.

- Swapping the thermostat
- Drilling anything
- Replacing blinds
- Anything on a shared wall or shared door
- Anything wired into the building

If they say no, use the Tier 1 alternative. There is almost always one.

## Exact Wording

### Tier 2 notification (smart lock)

> Hi [name] — quick heads up, not a request: I'm adding a smart lock to my unit. It replaces only the inside thumb turn of the existing deadbolt; the outside key cylinder and your key are untouched, and there are no holes. I'll refit the original thumb turn before I move out. Happy to show you if you'd like a look.

Why this works: it names the exact part being changed, confirms the landlord's key still works, states there are no holes, and promises restoration.

### Tier 2 notification (doorbell)

> Hi [name] — I'm putting a battery video doorbell on my own front door using an adhesive mount (no drilling, no wiring). It comes off clean when I leave. Let me know if you'd rather I didn't.

Note the last line. Offering an exit is what makes people comfortable.

### Tier 3 request (thermostat)

> Hi [name] — I'd like to install a smart thermostat in my unit at my own cost. I'd keep the original in a box and have it reinstalled before I move out, and I'd use a licensed HVAC tech so the system warranty isn't affected. Could you confirm in writing whether that's OK?

Three things make landlords say yes: **you pay**, **you restore**, and **a professional does the work**.

### Tier 3 request (drilling for a shelf or mount)

> Hi [name] — I'd like to mount [thing] on the wall in the living room. I'd use toggle bolts and patch and repaint the holes when I leave. Is that OK?

Expect a no, and have a no-drill alternative ready.

## What to Do If They Say No

Do not argue. Redirect to the Tier 1 version of the same benefit:

| They said no to | Do this instead |
|---|---|
| Smart lock | Keep the key; add a camera at the door |
| Video doorbell | Battery camera inside the entry, or on a shelf facing the door |
| Thermostat | SwitchBot Hub 2 or Sensibo over infrared |
| Wall-mounted camera | Shelf camera, or an adhesive mount on your own furniture |
| Smart blinds | Clip-on curtain robot on the existing rod |
| Drilling shelves | Freestanding shelving |

## Getting It in Writing (Without Being Awkward)

A text message is writing. So is an email. You do not need a contract amendment.

Save it. If a dispute ever comes up at move-out, a message saying "yes, that's fine" is what protects your deposit.

Practical steps:

1. Send the message from the number or address you already use with them
2. Screenshot the reply
3. Put the screenshots in a folder named `lease-2026`
4. Do not delete them when you move out — keep them for a year

## The Move-Out Checklist

This is what actually protects your deposit:

- [ ] Original thumb turn refitted to the deadbolt
- [ ] Doorbell removed, adhesive residue cleaned with rubbing alcohol
- [ ] All adhesive strips removed (warm them with a hair dryer first)
- [ ] Original thermostat reinstalled if you swapped it
- [ ] Original blinds rehung if you replaced them
- [ ] Every hole patched and painted if you drilled anything
- [ ] Photos of every wall, door and fixture, taken on move-out day

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I legally have to tell my landlord about a smart plug?**
No. A plug in your own outlet is not an alteration in any lease we have seen.

**Can my landlord ban smart home devices?**
They can ban alterations and fixtures. They cannot reasonably ban a lamp you plugged in. If a lease tries to ban "smart devices" wholesale, get it clarified in writing before signing.

**What if my landlord wants the smart lock removed mid-lease?**
Remove it. You have lost nothing — you keep the device and refit the original thumb turn in ten minutes. This is exactly why no-drill matters.

**Is a smart lock a security improvement my landlord should want?**
Usually yes, and it is worth saying so: auto-lock means the door is never left unlocked, and you can grant a one-time code to a contractor instead of handing over a key.

**Can I install a camera that points at a shared hallway?**
Check local law before you do. In most US states you cannot record audio without consent, and pointing a camera at a shared space raises privacy issues. Point it at your own door.
