# Moving With a Smart Home: The Complete Pack-Out and Reinstall Checklist

The whole point of a renter smart home is that it comes with you. But moving is exactly when people lose devices, forget to reset them, or leave a smart lock attached to a door they no longer rent.

This is the checklist we use. It is ordered by when you should do each thing, not by device type, because that is how a move actually goes.

## Four Weeks Before: Audit and Decide

### Make a device inventory

Walk the apartment and list everything that is smart. Include the things you forgot about.

- [ ] Smart plugs (count them — people forget the one behind the couch)
- [ ] Smart bulbs (count the sockets)
- [ ] Smart lock (inside half + any keypad)
- [ ] Video doorbell + its mount
- [ ] Cameras
- [ ] Hubs and bridges
- [ ] LED strips
- [ ] Curtain robots
- [ ] Sensors (contact, motion, leak)
- [ ] Thermostat (only if you installed it)
- [ ] Robot vacuum and its base
- [ ] Smart speakers

### Decide what to leave

Some things are not worth moving:

| Device | Move it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Adhesive LED strip | Usually no | Adhesive rarely survives; replacement is $25 |
| Cheap contact sensors | Yes | Small and reusable |
| Thermostat you installed | Yes, if you kept the original | Otherwise leave it and tell the landlord |
| Blinds you replaced | No — rehang the originals | They were the landlord's |
| Any device screwed to a wall | Yes, but patch the holes | You drilled them |

### Check your new place before you pack

Three questions that change what you keep:

1. **Is there a deadbolt?** No deadbolt means your smart lock does not fit.
2. **What kind of AC?** Window unit, mini-split, or central? It decides whether the IR blaster still works.
3. **Is the front door yours?** If it is a shared corridor with a landlord intercom, the doorbell stays behind.

## Two Weeks Before: Reset Everything

This is the step people skip, and it is the one that causes the most pain. **Reset every device to factory settings before you pack it**, because once it is in a box you will not remember which account it was on.

### Reset order (work backwards from the app)

1. **Remove each device from its app** — this unlinks it from your account and is what allows it to pair again
2. **Factory reset the hardware** — usually a 10-second hold on a button
3. **Delete the home/room** in the app if you are starting fresh
4. **Keep the account** — do not delete the Amazon, Google or Apple account, just the devices

### Device-specific reset notes

| Device | How to reset |
|---|---|
| TP-Link Kasa plug | Hold the button ~10s until the LED blinks amber/green |
| Wyze Lock | Remove from app, then hold the reset button in the battery compartment |
| Ring doorbell/camera | Hold the setup button ~20s |
| SwitchBot devices | Hold the button until the LED flashes, then remove from app |
| Aqara sensors | Hold the button ~10s; re-pair fresh at the new place |
| LIFX bulbs | Turn off/on 5 times in rhythm |
| Govee strip | Hold the controller button ~5s |

### Photograph the wiring before you undo it

If you installed a thermostat, photograph the wire terminals **before** you disconnect anything. The photo is worth more than any diagram.

## Moving Week: Pack It Properly

### The smart home box

Use one box, clearly labelled, and put **every** device in it. Not spread across three boxes — one box, so you cannot lose half a system.

Inside the box:

- [ ] Every device, reset and unlinked
- [ ] All power adapters and USB cables (label them with tape)
- [ ] Mounting brackets, screws, adhesive plates
- [ ] Original thumb turn for the smart lock
- [ ] Original thermostat (if you swapped it)
- [ ] Manuals, or a photo of each model number
- [ ] A printed inventory list taped to the inside of the lid

### Label every cable

Wrap a strip of masking tape around each adapter and write what it powers. In six weeks you will not remember which of five identical USB bricks ran the camera.

### Take photos of the empty walls

Before you leave, photograph every wall, door frame and fixture. This is your deposit evidence and it takes four minutes.

## Move-Out Day: Restore the Property

Do this before the final walkthrough, not after.

- [ ] Original thumb turn refitted to the deadbolt, smart lock in the box
- [ ] Doorbell removed, adhesive residue cleaned with rubbing alcohol
- [ ] All adhesive strips removed — warm them with a hair dryer first, then peel slowly
- [ ] Original thermostat reinstalled if you swapped it
- [ ] Original blinds rehung if you replaced them
- [ ] Every hole patched, sanded and painted
- [ ] Photos of everything, taken on the day

**On adhesive:** heat is the whole trick. A hair dryer on low for 30 seconds makes 3M strips release cleanly instead of taking paint with them. Dental floss behind the strip cuts the foam without pulling.

## Move-In Day: Reinstall in This Order

The order matters because each step depends on the last.

### 1. Internet first

Nothing works without Wi-Fi. Get the router up and confirm 2.4 GHz is broadcasting — most smart home devices cannot see a 5 GHz-only network, and a lot of modern routers ship with band steering that hides the 2.4 GHz SSID.

### 2. Hub and bridges second

If you have a hub (SwitchBot, Aqara, SmartThings), bring it up before the devices that depend on it.

### 3. Plugs and bulbs third

Fastest wins, and they confirm the network is working.

### 4. Lock fourth

Test the auto-unlock radius on a weekend, not on a work morning. Walk away 100 metres and come back three times.

### 5. Cameras and doorbell fifth

Mount last, after you know the Wi-Fi reaches the door. Weak signal at the front door is the single most common smart doorbell complaint.

### 6. Sensors last

Walk the apartment and test every window and door. This is the step where you find the sensor that fell off in the move.

## Re-Pairing Tips

- **Same account, new home.** Do not create a second account — just add devices to the existing one.
- **2.4 GHz only.** If pairing fails, temporarily split your Wi-Fi bands or disable 5 GHz for ten minutes.
- **Bluetooth for setup.** Most devices pair over Bluetooth then move to Wi-Fi. Keep your phone within a metre during setup.
- **One at a time.** Pairing five devices at once makes failures impossible to diagnose.
- **Matter devices need a controller.** If you moved from Alexa to Apple Home, check the device is Matter-certified before assuming it will work.

## What to Do With Devices You Are Not Reinstalling

- **Sell them.** Reset devices hold value well — a used smart lock sells for about half of new.
- **Give them away.** Better than a drawer.
- **Do not leave them.** A smart lock left on a door you no longer rent is a security problem for the next tenant and a liability for you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to reset devices before moving?**
Yes, if you want them to pair again cleanly. Devices still linked to an old account often refuse to pair, and the error message rarely says why.

**Will my smart lock fit the new apartment?**
Only if there is a deadbolt with a standard thumb turn on the inside. Measure the new door before you assume.

**Can I keep my security monitoring across a move?**
Yes for month-to-month plans (SimpliSafe, Ring, Abode). You may need to update the address and re-register the base station.

**What about a robot vacuum?**
It maps your old apartment. Delete the map and let it remap — an old map in a new home causes it to get lost and stop.

**How long does reinstall take?**
About three hours for a full $500 setup, spread over an evening and a morning. The lock and the doorbell take the longest.
