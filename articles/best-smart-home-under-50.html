What $50 Does NOT Buy

Be realistic about the limits:

  • No smart lock. Decent no-drill locks start at $89 (SwitchBot Lock Pro). Under $50, you're looking at basic Bluetooth locks that don't integrate with anything.
  • No security camera. Budget cameras exist ($25-35) but they're indoor-only and require a subscription for cloud storage. See our renter security camera guide for the full breakdown.
  • No thermostat control. Sensibo Sky at $49 is the cheapest option, and it only works with AC units that have remotes.
  • No smart blinds. Motorized blind kits start at $89 (SwitchBot Curtain 3).

$50 gets you lighting, plugs, and basic automation. For security, locks, and climate, you'll need to step up to the $100 starter kit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the single best smart home device under $50 for a renter? A smart plug. Specifically the TP-Link Kasa EP25 4-pack at $35. It's the highest-impact, lowest-cost device you can buy. Schedules, voice control, energy monitoring — all for under $9 per plug.

Can I build a smart home for under $50? Yes, but it's limited to lighting, plugs, and basic automation. You won't get security, locks, or climate control at this price. See our $100 starter kit for the next step up.

Do cheap smart home devices work with Alexa and Google Home? Most do. TP-Link Kasa, Wyze, Govee, and Amazon Smart Plug all work with both Alexa and Google Home. Sensibo Sky works with Alexa, Google Home, and Apple HomeKit. SwitchBot Bot needs the SwitchBot Hub for voice control.

Are cheap smart plugs safe? Yes, if you buy from reputable brands (TP-Link, Wyze, Amazon, Eve). Look for UL certification. Avoid no-name brands from random sellers — they can be fire hazards. All plugs recommended here are UL-listed.

Do I need a hub for any of these? No. Every device on this list connects directly to Wi-Fi. No hub, no bridge, no extra cost. The only exception is SwitchBot Bot, which needs the SwitchBot Hub for remote access and voice control (but works on a timer without it).
